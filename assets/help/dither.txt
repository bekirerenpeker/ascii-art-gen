asciigen -- dithering

Nudges pixel values before the glyph is chosen, so a limited set of characters
can suggest more tones than it really has. Off by default.

  --dither NAME                                                 (none)
      none | bayer4

  --dither-levels N                                                (4)
      How many tone steps the selector can resolve. This is the STRENGTH
      knob, and it runs backwards: LOWER dithers harder.
      4  strong     16  gentle     32  barely there

  --dither-adaptive on|off                                        (on)
      Back off where the picture already has detail.

  --dither-flat-contrast F                                        (10)
      Below this much local variation, dither at full strength.
      5  only the flattest sky     10  default     20  most of the picture

  --dither-edge-contrast F                                        (45)
      Above this, do not dither at all. Raise it to let dithering reach
      further into textured areas; lower it to protect more.
      25  protective     45  default     80  dither almost everywhere


  BOTH ARE MEASURED IN TONE LEVELS

  The number is the standard deviation of brightness in the neighbourhood,
  on the same 0-255 scale as the pixels. That makes them readable:

    2-8     clear sky, a painted wall, studio backdrop
    15-30   fabric, foliage, gravel, skin texture
    50+     a hard edge -- the boundary of an object

  So the defaults say: dither freely below 10, not at all above 45, and fade
  between. Anything in the middle gets a proportional amount.


LEAVE --dither-adaptive ON

  Dithering exists to break up banding, and banding only happens in smooth
  areas. Applied everywhere it shakes clean edges and fine detail apart.

  Measured on a photo: with the gate off, 39% of detail cells were disturbed
  and lines fragmented by 21%. With it on, 2% and 0% -- while flat areas
  stayed exactly as dithered either way. You lose nothing by leaving it on.


A NOTE ON DEEP BLACKS

  A tone sitting hard against black has nowhere to be dithered towards --
  half the nudge would invent brightness the photo never had, which is what
  speckles a flat background. That is handled automatically and is not a
  setting.
