asciigen -- source adjustments

Applied to the photo BEFORE glyphs are chosen, so they change WHICH GLYPH gets
picked. Use these to give the matcher a cleaner signal -- not to fix how the
output looks. For that, see --help grid.

Nothing here is on by default.

  --source-auto-levels [LO,HI]                            (off; 0.5,99.5)
      Stretch the tonal range so the darks reach black and the brights reach
      white. LO and HI are percentiles, not absolute values -- one blown
      highlight would otherwise pin the range and the stretch would do
      nothing.

      The best thing to reach for first. Biggest single improvement on a dull
      photo, and it needs no tuning.

      0,100     literal darkest and brightest pixel. One hot pixel or one
                blown highlight and the stretch does nothing
      0.5,99.5  default. Ignores the outliers, keeps everything real
      2,98      aggressive. Visibly crushes the extremes

  --source-levels B,W[,G]                                        (off)
      The manual version. Black point, white point, and optionally gamma.
      B and W are 0-255 tone values, not percentiles.

      20,235      mild stretch
      40,215,1.2  stronger, and lifted
      G  1.0 none    1.4 lifts    2.0 heavy lift    0.7 darkens

      CAREFUL: this gamma runs the OPPOSITE way to --grid-gamma. Here above
      1 brightens; there below 1 brightens. Different formulas, kept as they
      are because each matches the convention of its own stage.

  --source-contrast F                                            (1.0)
      Pivots around mid-grey. Below 1 flattens.
      0.8  flatter     1.0  off     1.3  noticeable     1.8  harsh, clips

  --source-sharpen A[,R]                                         (off)
      Unsharp mask: amount, and optionally radius. Helps --algo structure
      in particular, since a harder edge gives its direction term more to
      go on.

      A  0.3  subtle    0.6  strong (what --preset photo uses)
         1.0  crunchy   above 1.5  visible halos
      R  see RADII below. 1 is almost always right.

  --source-blur R                                                (off)
      Softens. Worth it on noisy photos, where grain competes with real
      structure for the glyph choice.

      1  denoise, shapes intact     2  visibly softer
      3  about one cell wide        4+  blurs across cells, goes mushy


RADII

  --source-sharpen and --source-blur take a radius in PLANE pixels -- the
  resolution the matcher works at, not the resolution of your photo. The
  photo is resampled down first, then filtered.

  That makes the radius mean the same thing on every picture. A 1920px photo
  and a 6900px photo at the same grid size get the same result from the same
  number, so a value that looked right once stays right.

  For scale: one CELL is --font-match-size pixels tall and half that wide, so
  8x16 plane pixels by default. A radius covers 2R+1 pixels, meaning

    R 1  ->  3px, about a third of a cell across
    R 3  ->  7px, roughly one whole cell
    R 4+ ->  wider than a cell; detail bleeds between neighbours

  Raising --font-match-size makes the plane bigger, so radii scale with it.
